# Round log

Every round: open the real site, criticise it against screenshots, change the code or the design, check again.
Tools: Playwright (the `playwright` MCP) against `next dev` (port 3000) and a production build served by `next start` (port 3200).
Nothing here is a score; each line is a specific problem, the change that answered it, and what the re-check showed.

## Direction change (after round 1 of the first design)

The first design was a long single page with a feature list, an explorer and a credits block. The developer asked for the opposite:
minimal, easy to understand, far less text; smooth transitions everywhere; a carousel; the product's own avatars as the storytelling; and the deep detail on a
separate page laid out like the app's Settings. The first design's round-1 findings are kept below because the fixes carried over; the rounds after
that apply to the new design.

## Notes from the article the developer sent (Utsubo, immersive storytelling)

Used: beginning–middle–end rather than a page hierarchy (hook → the rule → the real app → the climax → the proof → the way on),
at least three genuine interactions (pick an avatar, swipe the moods, drag the divider, turn the core sample and move the slider), transitions treated as
scenes (the dark section grows out of the page), a palette that follows the story (the carousel is tinted by each avatar's colour).
Not used: sound (the article calls it "not optional"; here it would be unrequested audio on a page people open in a shared room), gyroscope tilt.
The article says nothing about accessibility; the standard of this site is its own.
